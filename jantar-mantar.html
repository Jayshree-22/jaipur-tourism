About Jantar Mantar

Jantar Mantar is a collection of 19 fixed instruments for observing the sky with the naked eye. They are built of stone and lime plaster on a very large scale so that small movements of the Sun and stars can be read accurately.

The Samrat Yantra is a giant sundial whose shadow moves visibly across its curved scales, giving local time to within about two seconds. Nearby, the Rashivalaya instruments each track one of the twelve zodiac signs.

History

  1. 1724Jai Singh II begins building observatories, starting in Delhi.
  2. 1734The Jaipur observatory is completed; it is the largest of his five.
  3. Early 1900sThe site is restored after years of neglect.
  4. 2010UNESCO lists Jantar Mantar as an outstanding example of pre-telescope astronomy.

What to look for

  • Samrat Yantra and its moving shadow
  • Jai Prakash Yantra, the twin bowls
  • The twelve Rashivalaya instruments
  • Chakra Yantra, the ring instrument
